## What “verified” means here

BridgePoint does not use the word verified to mean that a roof has been physically inspected or that damage is confirmed. In this context, verified means the opportunity passed BridgePoint’s current evidence and quality gates for the roofing vertical, including direct-source evidence and independent evidence-family requirements.

A contractor still needs normal field verification before making any statement about actual roof condition, damage, repair need or claim eligibility.

## Why the concentration matters

The practical value of the market view is prioritization. A roofing company deciding where to focus limited sales or inspection capacity can start with markets where multiple property-level candidates have already cleared the same strict evidence gate rather than treating every address in Connecticut as equally likely to deserve attention.

That does not eliminate judgment. It gives the team a narrower, evidence-backed place to begin.

## Freshness matters

The current dataset also shows why counts should not be used without dates. Torrington has the largest candidate count, but Vernon and Burlington contain more recent direct evidence in the present set. New Britain and Middlebury include older evidence, so BridgePoint treats those records as historical context rather than pretending every candidate is equally fresh.

## What BridgePoint is trying to solve

Traditional territory selection often begins with a storm map, a broad demographic list, a purchased lead list or manual research. BridgePoint’s approach is to connect property identity, source provenance, timing, evidence families and opportunity logic so a contractor can answer a more useful question:

**Which properties deserve human attention first, and why?**

That is different from claiming that software can inspect a roof remotely.

### Important limitation

A BridgePoint opportunity is a prioritization signal, **not proof of property damage, insurance coverage, claim validity, repair need or contractor eligibility to perform work**. Field inspection and professional judgment remain necessary.
