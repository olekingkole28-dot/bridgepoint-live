Insurance claim delay benchmarks: why disputed-claim timeline duration is a missing data layer

A methodology for measuring verified stage-by-stage claim duration without turning observational data into unsupported causal claims.
Published August 22, 2026 · Automatic public research distribution · Source content passed BridgePoint's customer-safety quality gate.

Claim files contain many dates, but those dates are rarely normalized into a reusable, comparable timeline across organizations. That makes it difficult to answer basic operational questions such as how long inspection-to-estimate, supplement-to-response, dispute-to-appraisal or settlement-to-payment actually took.

BridgePoint is building a verified event model for these milestones. Each contributed timeline must retain event type, date and verification state. Aggregate benchmarks should be generated only from verified events and only when the cohort is large enough to protect privacy and avoid thin conclusions.

For right-censored claims that are still open, time-to-event methods can provide a more honest picture than pretending every open file has a final duration. Company case studies should disclose sample size, study period, inclusion and exclusion rules, median durations, uncertainty and limitations.

A measured before-versus-after difference can be useful evidence, but it should not automatically be described as causation. BridgePoint's goal is to create defensible real-world timing data that can eventually show where delay accumulates and whether workflow changes are associated with better outcomes.

Use limitation: BridgePoint public research is decision-support. It does not prove property damage, claim coverage, customer intent, a permit, or a guaranteed outcome.
